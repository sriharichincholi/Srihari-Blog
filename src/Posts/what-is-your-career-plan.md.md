Hello, I’m Srihari! 

I’m an 18-year-old high school graduate who recently started my undergraduate degree in Information Technology. When I’m not studying, you’ll usually find me playing video games, building (or breaking) software, or practicing music.

I’m writing this blog as an official answer to a question every neighborhood uncle or relative at a family gathering has asked me: *"What is your career plan? Where do you want to go next?"*

To give a bit of context: I’ve always had an appetite for STEM. I got exposed to computers at a young age—waiting for new *Angry Birds* updates or *Need for Speed* releases, playing as a typical free-to-play gamer. Naturally, those early exposures steered me toward engineering. 

I was fortunate to stay in the top 8 students of my Institution in 10th grade, win STEM Olympiad medals from school to district levels, and clear competitive exams like JEE Mains and TG EAPCET (EAMCET), where I secured a rank of 3,700—something I’m deeply grateful to God and my parents for.

So, why Quant?

For a long time, my default plan was game development. But I noticed a pattern in how I worked: I’d love building at first, hit an extraordinarily tough boulder, fight it, rage-quit, and eventually hit a wall. 

Quantitative finance introduced a completely different dynamic. 

Initially, I assumed quant was just corporate finance and stock management. But once I realized it involves world-class engineers building low-latency algorithms to trim execution delays down to microseconds—and even nanoseconds—it clicked instantly: **If not here, I don't think I’d be happier anywhere else.**

It combines high-performance C++ systems engineering with advanced statistical research (often at a post-doctoral level). Research has always fascinated me because it offers genuine autonomy—your domain of inquiry can be completely distinct from the researcher next to you in the lab.

I know this might look like a *"small guy, big dreams"* situation. But I’m committed to building the knowledge, rigor, and terms to back it up.

Wishing myself the best of luck.

— **Srihari**
